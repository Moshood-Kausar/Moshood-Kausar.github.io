I'm a Master's/PhD student in Computer Science at Oregon State University, part of the Advanced Software Engineering Research (ANSWER) Lab and the High-Performance Computing and Systems Group (HiPCastor). I work in automated software engineering and testing, applying fuzzing, delta debugging and verification techniques to localize faults in complex systems.

Alongside that, I work on speech and language technology for low-resource African languages with LyngualLabs, covering Yoruba TTS accessibility, ASR for code-switched speech and multilingual speech recognition.

I hold a bachelor's degree in Computer Engineering from Oduduwa University, where I graduated with first-class honors as the university's top overall student. Outside research, I lead the AWS Student Builder Group at Oregon State University as its first Student Lead, and I coordinate community tech events (I was previously a GDG on Campus Lead and have spoken at 50+ RAIN-INNetwork events across Nigeria), and I recently won 1st place in the Social Impact track at BeaverHacks 2026 with SpeakAbroad, a conversation-practice platform for international students.

Testing · HPC code translation
Kaizen: Metamorphic Fuzzing and Differential Testing for LLM-Translated HPC Applications
Oscar Ludwig, Ninad Anklesaria, Zheming Jin, Swaroop Pophale, Kausar Moshood, Christian J. DeVore, Brandon Gill, Cassius Villareal, Keita Teranishi, Manish Motwani
arXiv preprint, 2026
what it does
LLMs are increasingly used to port scientific code across HPC programming models (CUDA to OpenMP, OpenACC, Kokkos, SYCL), but compilation success and token similarity can't confirm the translation is actually correct. Kaizen uses metamorphic fuzzing and differential testing to catch behavioral divergences that static benchmarks miss.

Speech · edge ASR
WAXAL-NET: Finetuned Edge ASR Across 19 African Languages
Victor Tolulope Olufemi, Oreoluwa Babatunde, Ramsey Njema, Bolarinwa Gbotemi, Wanchi Lucia Yen, John Uzodinma, Sunday Ajayi, Oluwademilade Williams, Kausar Moshood, et al.
arXiv preprint, 2026
what it does
Tests whether compact, domain-specialized ASR models can beat massively multilingual foundation models on conversational African speech across 19 languages. Finetuned edge models cut macro-averaged WER from 64.9% to 38.0% versus the best zero-shot baseline, using models 3–40× smaller.

Accessibility · TTS
Evaluating Yoruba Text-to-Speech Systems for Accessible Computer-Based Testing in Visually Impaired Learners
Kausar Moshood, Victor Olufemi, Oreoluwa Babatunde, Emmanuel Bolarinwa, Williams Oluwademilade
AfricaNLP 2026 (7th Workshop on African NLP), pp. 228–234
what it does
Tests whether current Yoruba TTS systems can deliver standardized exam content to visually impaired students. Five blind students compared human narration, Braille, and TTS (Facebook's mms-tts-yor and YarnGPT); all preferred Braille, exposing a real gap between TTS capability and what low-resource-language accessibility actually needs.

Speech · code-switched ASR
Closing the Gap in Low-Resource ASR: Leveraging Multilingual Models for Code-Switched Yoruba-English Speech
Emmanuel Bolarinwa, Oreoluwa Babatunde, Victor Olufemi, Kausar Moshood, Oluwademilade Williams
Deep Learning Indaba 2025 Research Track, PMLR 302:1–9
what it does
Evaluates Whisper Large v3 and MMS-1B-All on Yoruba-English code-switched speech, then finetunes both on a 21-hour CS dataset. Finetuning cuts word error rate substantially: 55.8% for MMS-1B-All and 50.1% for Whisper Large v3, showing multilingual ASR models can be adapted for code-switched, low-resource scenarios.
